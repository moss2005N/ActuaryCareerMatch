# Licensing FAQ (draft)

ActuaryCareerMatch is **source-available**, not OSI-approved open source. The
controlling terms are in [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE). This FAQ explains the intended
policy but does not amend the license or provide legal advice. If the FAQ and
license conflict, the license controls.

The custom draft has not received independent legal review. It must not be
described as legally approved, legally valid, open source, or OSI-approved open

## How do downstream recipients receive permission?

Each recipient receives permission for Dawei Niu's Original Software directly
from Dawei Niu under the license. A person who redistributes a compliant copy is
not sublicensing or transferring their own license.

For an independently authored modification, each downstream recipient receives
permission directly from that modification's copyright holder under Section 3.
The person distributing a Modified Work may distribute only material they own or
are authorized to distribute.

## Who owns and can commercially license modifications?

Dawei Niu retains copyright in the Original Software. An independent author
retains copyright in their Contribution. Distributing a Contribution under the
project license grants direct non-commercial rights to recipients; it does not
assign the Contribution to Dawei Niu or authorize him to license it commercially.

Dawei Niu may commercially license only code he owns or for which he has
separately obtained sufficient rights. Commercial use of a Modified Work may
require separate agreements with both Dawei Niu and applicable Contributors.
The current contribution policy accepts reports and suggestions only, not code
or other copyrightable pull-request contributions.

The license itself is a custom draft and must be reviewed by a qualified lawyer
before the repository is made public or the terms are relied upon.

## What is allowed without separate permission?

- Viewing and downloading the source code.
- Personal learning and local experimentation that is not connected to a
  business or revenue-generating activity.
- Non-commercial teaching, coursework, and educational projects.
- Non-commercial academic research, subject to the funding and sponsor caveats
  below.
- A local personal portfolio demonstration, or screenshots and recordings of
  it, when no running instance is made available to other people and it is not
  monetized.
- Sharing the original code or modifications for non-commercial purposes when
  attribution, the license, source availability, and a notice of changes are
  preserved.

## What requires prior written permission?

- Any public hosting or deployment of the Original Software or a Modified Work,
  including a free, nonprofit, educational, research, portfolio, demonstration,
  preview, or test instance. Lack of payment or revenue does not create hosting
  permission.
- Any deployment, internal use, evaluation, modification, integration, or
  operation by or for a for-profit business. A free proof of concept can still
  create commercial advantage.
- A hosted service operated as part of a business, even if end users are not
  charged directly.
- Selling the Original Software or a Modified Work, charging for access or support, advertising,
  sponsorship, lead generation, or consulting and client work involving it.
- Incorporating any part of the Software into a commercial product or service.
- Sponsored research or education when a commercial sponsor receives rights,
  privileged access, a deliverable, or another commercial benefit.

Commercial permission for the Original Software must come from Dawei Niu in a
separate signed written agreement. Commercial use of a Modified Work may also
require separate permission from the copyright holders of its Contributions.
Repository access or silence is not permission.

## Ambiguous situations

### Is all university use non-commercial?

No. Ordinary teaching and independent academic research may qualify even when
participants receive salaries, tuition is charged, or public grants fund the
institution. Contract research, commercialization work, or sponsor-controlled
deliverables may be commercial. Review mixed-purpose projects individually.

### Is all nonprofit or government use permitted?

No automatic conclusion should be drawn from organizational status. A charity,
government body, or public-benefit organization can participate in a commercial
arrangement, and a contractor may be acting for commercial advantage. Obtain
written permission when the purpose or beneficiaries are mixed or unclear.

### May I accept donations or recover hosting costs?

The draft does not promise that donations, sponsorship, or cost recovery are
non-commercial. Recurring donations tied to access, sponsorship benefits, or
amounts beyond unavoidable direct costs are especially likely to require
permission. Ask before collecting money in connection with the Software.

### May a company inspect the repository?

Yes, anyone may view and download it. A for-profit organization needs written
permission before it runs, evaluates, modifies, deploys, or integrates the code.

### May I publish a fork?

You may publish and redistribute the fork's source code only for a
non-commercial purpose and only if you retain the license and attribution,
identify your changes and their date, and provide corresponding source code.
You may not add terms that expand or restrict recipients' rights. This
source-redistribution permission does not permit you to run the fork as a
publicly accessible website, service, demo, preview, or test environment.

### May I host a free public demo for a class, research project, or portfolio?

No, not without prior written authorization. The draft treats making a running
instance available to the public or external users as Public Hosting or
Deployment even when it is free, has no advertising, and has an educational,
research, portfolio, or other non-commercial purpose. Local private use remains
permitted, and screenshots or recordings may be shared when they do not provide
access to a running instance.

### Does this cover dependencies, data, names, or logos?

No. Third-party job postings and descriptions, employer-authored content,
company and product names, trademarks, service marks, logos, externally sourced
datasets, linked content, dependencies, libraries, assets, and separately
licensed components are excluded from the licensed Original Software and
Contributions. They remain subject to applicable law and their own terms.

The limited use of the ActuaryCareerMatch name required for attribution is not a
trademark license. Attribution and identification of a Modified Work do not imply
endorsement, sponsorship, or affiliation. The draft grants no express patent
license.

## Issues requiring owner and legal review

Before publication, a qualified lawyer should review at least:

1. whether the stated copyright holder and monitored licensing contact are the
   appropriate legal party and notice details for licensing agreements;
2. whether the Commercial Use and Non-Commercial Purpose definitions match the
   owner's intended treatment of nonprofits, government, grants, tuition,
   donations, portfolio demonstrations, public hosting, and sponsored research;
3. governing law, venue, local consumer-law limits, enforceability, termination,
   warranty, and liability language;
4. whether an express patent grant or patent-retaliation clause is appropriate;
5. compatibility with every dependency, bundled asset, fixture, and data source;
6. whether the direct downstream Contribution grant is effective in all relevant
   jurisdictions and whether future code contributions require assignments or a
   separate contributor agreement for commercial relicensing; and
7. use of the project's name and any trademark policy; and
8. whether the distinction between permitted source redistribution and
   prohibited Public Hosting or Deployment is sufficiently definite and
   enforceable in every relevant jurisdiction.

Do not describe the project as “open source.” “Source-available under a custom
non-commercial license” is the accurate description while these terms apply.
